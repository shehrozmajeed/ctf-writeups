## Overview

This room introduces the core concepts of AI/ML security by covering how AI systems are built, the terminology used across the field, and — most importantly — how these systems can be attacked and defended. It serves as a foundation for understanding the unique threat landscape that AI and machine learning introduce compared to traditional software.

## Key Concepts Learned

- **AI Building Blocks** — the relationship between data, models, training, and inference, and why each stage introduces its own attack surface.
- **LLM Fundamentals** — how large language models process and generate text, forming the basis for later prompt-injection and adversarial testing rooms.
- **Offensive AI Threats** — common attack categories against AI/ML systems, including:
  - Data poisoning
  - Model evasion
  - Model extraction / theft
  - Adversarial inputs
  - Prompt injection (LLM-specific)
- **Defensive AI** — how AI/ML techniques are applied on the defensive side (e.g., anomaly detection, automated triage) and the trade-offs involved.
- **Dual-Use Nature of AI** — the same underlying technology that powers defensive tooling can be weaponized by attackers, reinforcing the need for security-aware AI development.

## Takeaway

This room provided the conceptual groundwork needed to reason about AI as both an attack surface and a defensive tool — a prerequisite for deeper, hands-on AI security rooms (model security, prompt engineering, adversarial testing).
