## What I Learned

My first hands-on defensive exercise — playing the SOC analyst role protecting a simulated bank from a live attack.

**Thinking like a defender:** Where offense looks for one way in, defense has to cover every possible way in — a fundamentally asymmetric challenge. Defenders win by detecting and responding quickly, not by being perfect.

**Detecting suspicious activity:** Reviewed logs for anomalies — repeated failed logins, unusual request patterns, traffic from unexpected sources.

**Identifying the attack:** Correlated the suspicious activity into a specific attack type (rather than just noting "something looks wrong") — this is the difference between raw log noise and an actionable security incident.

**Stopping the attack:** Took the actual remediation step — blocking the malicious source — to end the ongoing compromise.

---

## Key Takeaway

This room was a direct mirror of the Offensive Security Intro room — same skillset, opposite side of the table. Understanding both perspectives is what makes a SOC analyst effective: you defend better when you know how an attacker thinks, and you attack more realistically when you understand what defenders are watching for.
